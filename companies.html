Sister Companies

Four operating entities, one unified platform.

XTOMORROW does not operate alone. It coordinates a network of sister companies, each specialized in one layer of the integrated capital-to-asset stack — from issuance and capital markets, to operating concessions, to processing technology, to Sharia-compliant capital pools.

01

XTOMORROW LLC

Formed 29 October 2025 (New Mexico). The capital-formation, issuance, and investor-relations entity. Operates the website, the strategic-investor program, and the multi-jurisdiction STO issuance pipeline. Executive offices in Dallas, Texas.

02

Independent Energy Partners (iEP US LLC + iEP Argentina S.A.)

The operating partner for sovereign-scale concessions. Holds the 60-year public-private partnership with YMAD for the 54,000-hectare Peirano Block in Catamarca, Argentina. Manages on-the-ground operations, environmental compliance, and government relationships.

03

Bober Khan Wealth Holdings Pte. Ltd. (BKWH)

Singapore SPV behind the BKGT (Bober Khan Gold Token). UEN 202430326M. Issues a fully-backed, Sharia-compliant, MRHB-attested digital gold instrument on Lumia zkEVM L2. Anchors XTOMORROW's GCC and Asian Sharia-compliant capital pool.

04

Geoenergy Solutions

Technology subsidiary of BKWH. Owns and licenses the PCE and EDMSU platforms — a cyanide-free, low-temperature, water-light extraction stack validated at KGHM Polska Miedź (2013), ENAMI Chile (2020), and Elemental Poland (2025).

Coordination model

One unified vision. Four operating tracks.

Each entity has a discrete legal personality, separate audit, and independent compliance — but operates under a unified capital-allocation framework. Combining the speed of a single operator with the regulatory and tax efficiency of multiple jurisdictional vehicles.

  • Asset risk isolated at the operating-entity level.
  • Jurisdictional optimization for tax, regulatory, and Sharia compliance.
  • Independent audit per sister company strengthens institutional acceptance.
  • Cross-entity capital flow managed through arms-length intercompany agreements.
